Introduction

Welcome to IS 201!

In this course, we will explore the ethical questions that arise from the development and use of computers, digital technologies, and artificial intelligence.

We will examine not only what technology can do, but also what we should do as computing professionals, developers, researchers, and users of technology. Throughout the semester, we will discuss topics such as professional responsibility, privacy and digital rights, cyberethics, AI ethics, software development ethics, equity and social justice, technology governance, responsible innovation, and sustainable computing.

More importantly, this course is about learning to think critically about the consequences of our technological choices. Many ethical issues in computing do not have simple right-or-wrong answers. You will be encouraged to examine different perspectives, justify your decisions, and consider how technology affects individuals, communities, and society.

Like most courses at the UP Open University, IS 201 is conducted asynchronously. There are no scheduled Zoom classes or fixed class hours. You may study at your own pace, following the course schedule and deadlines. All course materials, activities, submissions, and discussions are available on our MyPortal course site.
Course Info

Learning Outcomes

After completing this course, you should be able to:

  1. Define ethics and explain major ethical theories and approaches.
  2. Define computer ethics and explain its relevance to computing and information technology.
  3. Describe the philosophical challenges involved in computer ethics.
  4. Analyze ethical problems arising from computers, digital technologies, and the wider world of information technology.
  5. Reflect on your own ethical responsibilities as an IT professional and as a member of an online community.
Read Carefully

What Your Professor Expects From You

As a UPOU student — you should be able to learn independently. Take ownership of your learning. The asynchronous format gives you flexibility, but it also requires self-discipline and initiative. There is no spoon-feeding here — engage with the materials, use the available resources, and take responsibility for your progress.
As a UP student — you should uphold academic integrity. Your work should reflect your own thinking, understanding, and effort. Cheating, plagiarism, and other forms of academic dishonesty will not be condoned.
As a Master's student — you should demonstrate academic maturity. Go beyond memorization. Critically analyze concepts, synthesize different perspectives, apply theories to real-world situations, and contribute meaningful insights.
As a Computer Ethics student — you should think ethically and critically about technology. Ethical issues in computing rarely have simple answers. Examine different perspectives, consider consequences, question your assumptions, and justify your positions through sound ethical reasoning.
As a human being — you should demonstrate respect and professionalism at all times. Maintain respectful and professional communication in all forums, emails, and class discussions.

Deliverables

Course Requirements

10%

End-of-Topic Quizzes

Individual automated quizzes administered at the end of selected topics. The quizzes assess understanding of key concepts, ethical frameworks, professional responsibilities, and issues discussed in the course.

25%

Midterm Report

A written group report analyzing a selected computer ethics issue or case. The report should identify key ethical concerns and stakeholders, apply relevant ethical frameworks, and present a well-supported position. All groups submit a Midterm Report.

25%

Final Report

Another written group report providing an in-depth ethical analysis of a contemporary computing issue, technology, system, practice, or policy. The report should integrate concepts from the course and provide evidence-based recommendations. All groups submit a Final Report.

40%

Seminar Presentation

Each group prepares one recorded seminar presentation based on either its Midterm or Final Report. Half of the groups participate in the Midterm Seminar, the rest in the Final Seminar. All group members must contribute meaningfully; non-presenting students engage through the designated discussion activities.

Class Policies & Guidelines

Academic Integrity Policy

Academic integrity is fundamental to this course. All submitted work should reflect your own thinking, understanding, and effort, with appropriate acknowledgment of the ideas and contributions of others.

Properly cite all sources used in forum posts, reports, presentations, and other course requirements. Include a reference list in APA style where applicable. When using AI tools, follow the course's AI Usage Policy, particularly its requirements on transparency, verification, and responsible use.

Students are expected to understand and observe the University's standards on academic integrity. If you are uncertain about proper citation, attribution, collaboration, or acceptable use of sources, consult your professor before submitting your work.

Cheating, plagiarism, fabrication, misrepresentation, and other forms of academic dishonesty may result in failure of the requirement, failure of the course, and/or further disciplinary action under University rules.
Class Policies & Guidelines

AI Usage Policy

This course adopts an AI-positive approach.

Students may freely use AI tools to support learning and problem solving. The use of AI is permitted and will not be penalized, provided that it is ethical, transparent, and consistent with course requirements.

AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT, Google Gemini, Grammarly, Google Translate) can be valuable aids, but they are not infallible. LLMs may produce inaccurate or fabricated information, while writing and translation tools may distort meaning or omit important context. Approach all AI-generated outputs with appropriate skepticism and critical judgment.

Any AI-generated content should be carefully reviewed, verified, and meaningfully integrated into the final output. Students remain fully responsible for any AI-generated outputs they use or submit. Work that relies primarily on AI without substantial personal input is unacceptable.

When AI tools are used for a course requirement, students should document the tool(s) used, how they were used, and the rationale for their use. This promotes transparency, accountability, and academic integrity.

Respect and Conduct Policy

IS 201 is a Respect Zone. Maintain respectful and professional communication in all course interactions, including forums, emails, and discussions. Address your classmates, tutors, and professor properly, using appropriate titles and names, and engage with differing perspectives courteously and constructively.

Students are expected to observe appropriate netiquette and help maintain a learning environment where everyone can participate meaningfully. Forum posts may be moderated or removed when they are off-topic, disruptive, inappropriate, or inconsistent with these expectations.

Cyberviolence and other forms of serious misconduct, including insults, threats, harassment, discrimination, and hate speech, will not be tolerated. Serious violations may be referred to the Office of Student Affairs for appropriate action.
